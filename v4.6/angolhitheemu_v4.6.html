PIR senses changes in infrared radiation. Warm moving bodies trigger it best; vehicle detection is not reliable in all conditions. Events come from the simulated road users or the test button, never from real sensors. The IR scenarios in Nasih.pdf are not validation of this six-PIR design.

Follow-me lighting Harbour only

Wake pattern
Lamps lit ahead Input 2
Tail hold behind the road user Input 2 s
PIR cannot identify anyone or tell direction from one pole. The first pole to see a road user wakes both sides; once a neighbouring pole detects next, the direction is known, lamps ahead light in sequence, the lamp just passed dims after the tail hold, and the lamp ahead stays lit for the estimated time to arrive (pole gap ÷ measured speed). Several people close together look like one track. Arrows on the road show each track.

Acceptance checks

Manual verification procedure
  1. View › Harbour smart poles at 21:00. Only harbour poles show sensor fans; zoom to any other lamp: no fans, constant 100 %.
  2. Lighting › Close-up of a smart pole: count six lenses on the 914 mm ring and the rear controller box; switch x-ray on to see the wiring.
  3. Pause at 23:00 with the harbour empty: harbour lamps settle at the standby level.
  4. Select a harbour pole, press Inject test event: that pole rises to 100 %, neighbours follow 0.35 s apart, nothing else changes; after the hold they fade back to standby.
  5. Set 12:00 and inject again: the log shows "day: no lighting action".
  6. Sensor › 20 lux, Manual ambient ≈ 20 lx, fluctuation 40 %: with band 0 and dwell 0 the harbour flickers; with defaults it holds. Other lamps follow only the clock.
  7. Change the lamp rating: harbour installed power = poles × rating, and live power scales with it.
